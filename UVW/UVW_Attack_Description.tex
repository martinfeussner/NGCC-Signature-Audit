\begin{abstract}
UVW is a ternary hash-and-sign scheme based on a generalized
$(U+V,U+W)$ code.  A fixed secret monomial transformation is intended to hide
the two public coordinates derived from each secret coordinate.  The UVW
$XY$ decoder nevertheless leaves a persistent statistical fingerprint on
each hidden pair: for a fresh information set of 3,250 secret positions, it
forces both coordinates in every selected pair to be nonzero.  A signature
transmits only the last half of a systematic error, but the first half can be
recovered exactly from the public syndrome and public key.  Ordinary
signatures therefore expose repeated samples of all 9,700 public coordinates.

We score coordinate pairs using a likelihood model derived from the public
parameters.  Mutual nearest neighbors provide high-confidence pair and
relative-scale candidates, and only enough such candidates to span a
1,600-dimensional public subspace are required.  Quotienting the public-key
columns by this subspace then partitions all 9,700 columns into the 4,850
hidden pairs and determines their relative scales.  A public change of syndrome basis exposes matrices of the
form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
   \widetilde H_X&\widetilde H_Y\\
   -\widetilde H_Z&\widetilde H_Z
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The submitted $Z$ and $XY$ decoders can then be applied to these matrices,
yielding an equivalent signing key without recovering the original seed.

On one UVW-128 key, 300 genuine chosen-message signatures from the submitted
reference signer were sufficient.  The top 1,600 public candidates spanned
the required subspace, the quotient partition uniquely identified every pair,
and the resulting decoder produced a 1,244-byte submitted-API signature on a
message absent from the query set.  The intended mathematical verifier
accepted it with weight 8,633.  Four parallel signing processes collected the
transcript in 47.53 minutes on a four-core Intel Core i5-6500.  Public recovery and one
forgery took 57.50 seconds and at most 153 MiB.  At 200 signatures the same
fixed rule failed its public quotient certificate, so no secret-dependent
stopping decision is required.

This demonstrates a successful fresh-message forgery for one fixed UVW-128
key.  Only one key and one parameter set have undergone end-to-end testing on
a genuine source transcript, so the experiment does not estimate a
key-averaged success probability.  The attack targets the written
sampling method and intended verification equation; it is independent of the separately reported
accept-all API wrapper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

UVW is an NGCC code-based signature submission over $\F_3$ \cite{uvw-spec}.
Its secret parity-check matrix has a two-coordinate block structure, and a
fixed monomial map hides both the block matching and the nonzero coordinate
scales.  Security therefore requires ordinary signatures not to reveal which
public coordinates originate from the same secret block.

The signing procedure does not achieve that independence.  Its $XY$ decoder
selects an information set $I$ of size $k_1$.  For every $j\in I$, it chooses $e_{1,j}$
so that both entries of
\[
                   (e_{1,j},\,e_{1,j}+e_{2,j})
\]
are nonzero.  The same hidden coordinate pairing is used for every signature
under a key.  Rejection on the final Hamming weight controls the total number
of zeros, but it does not make their locations independent across the two
members of a hidden pair.

The attack therefore has a statistical front end followed by an exact
algebraic back end.  Pairwise likelihood scores first identify a set of reliable pairs.
Correctly oriented sums of their public-key columns lie in a fixed subspace $U$ of
dimension
\[
                   r_1=\frac n2-k_1=1600.
\]
Once $U$ is known, no further statistical decisions are needed.  In the
quotient $\F_3^{4850}/U$, the two columns of every hidden pair are the same
projective point.  The quotient then recovers all remaining pairs and their
relative scales, including pairs whose empirical likelihood evidence is weak.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item We identify the pairwise distribution leak in the specified UVW
        signing sampler and show how to reconstruct each complete public
        error from an ordinary signature.
  \item We give a public likelihood score for high-confidence pairs and an
        exact quotient method that completes the entire matching from a
        basis of $U$.
  \item We prove that the recovered pairing and scales yield an equivalent
        UVW signing key.
  \item We give a public stopping certificate and distinguish the heuristic
        likelihood model from the exact algebraic completion.
  \item We demonstrate the attack end to end on 300 genuine signatures from
        one submitted UVW-128 key and obtain an intended-verifier-accepted
        signature on a fresh literal message.
\end{itemize}

The public UVW signature tracker lists an accept-all wrapper and a verifier
crash \cite{ngcc-sign}.  Neither issue is used here.  We invoke the internal
mathematical verifier and require its syndrome and weight checks to pass.

\paragraph{Related UVW-KEM work.}
A public UVW-KEM reaction-attack lead recovers hidden column pairs and ratios
from about 1.06 million precise first-attempt reaction labels, then applies a
public projection and generalized Reed--Solomon structure
\cite{ngcc-kem,kem-forum}.  Its high-volume phase uses secret-assisted
simulation, and no black-box reaction classifier is demonstrated.  The
present signature attack uses the ordinary signing distribution, 300 real
source executions, a different 4,850-pair structure, and a quotient
completion followed by the signature decoders.  The KEM work is nevertheless
structurally related, so we do not claim that this report is the first use of
hidden UVW pairs or a cancelling public projection.  The signature attack was
developed before that KEM report was consulted.

\section{UVW-128}

Set
\[
 m=\frac n2=4850,\qquad
 r_1=m-k_1=1600,\qquad
 r_2=m-k_2=3250.
\]
The UVW-128 parameter tuple is
\[
        (q,n,k,k_1,k_2,w)=(3,9700,4850,3250,1600,8633).
\]
Before the secret monomial map, the parity-check matrix has the form
\begin{equation}
 H_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  H_X&H_Y\\
  -H_Z&H_Z
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:h0}
\end{equation}
where $H_X,H_Y\in\F_3^{r_1\times m}$ and
$H_Z\in\F_3^{r_2\times m}$.  Key generation applies an invertible row map and
a monomial column map, then publishes the systematic matrix
\begin{equation}
              H_{\pk}=(I_m\mid R).
 \label{eq:systematic}
\end{equation}

For a syndrome expressed in the secret row basis as
$(s_1\mid s_2)$, the trapdoor decoder first solves
\begin{align}
 e_2H_Z^{\mathsf T}&=s_2,\label{eq:decz}\\
 e_1(H_X+H_Y)^{\mathsf T}
     &=s_1-e_2H_Y^{\mathsf T}.\label{eq:decxy}
\end{align}
The structured error before the monomial map is
\begin{equation}
                    (e_1\mid e_1+e_2).
 \label{eq:structured}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Every signature exposes the complete public error}

A serialized UVW signature contains a salt and only the last $k=m$ entries
$v$ of the public error.  Let $s=\mathcal H(M,r)\in\F_3^m$ be the public hash
syndrome.  Writing the complete error as $(u\mid v)$, equations
\eqref{eq:systematic} and $H_{\pk}$ give
\begin{equation}
             s=(u\mid v)H_{\pk}^{\mathsf T}
              =u+vR^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
             u=s-vR^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:reconstruct}
\end{equation}
Thus the compact signature does not prevent an attacker from reconstructing
all 9,700 public coordinates of the structured error.

\section{Pairwise leakage}

\subsection{A rigorous support fact}

Algorithm~5 of the specification chooses an information set
$I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ of size $k_1$ and requires
\[
        \wt(e_{1,I})=\wt((e_1+e_2)_I)=k_1.
\]
The reference implementation realizes this rule by setting
\begin{equation}
 e_{1,j}=
 \begin{cases}
   e_{2,j},&e_{2,j}\ne0,\\
   \text{uniform in }\{1,2\},&e_{2,j}=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad j\in I.
 \label{eq:forced}
\end{equation}
Both entries of pair $j$ are therefore nonzero whenever $j\in I$.  This is
an exact support property and does not rely on a random-code approximation.
The final fixed-weight rejection can alter other probabilities, but it cannot
introduce a zero into a selected pair.

\subsection{A heuristic likelihood model}

The likelihood model used in the experiment depends only on public
parameters.  It approximates affine-solved coordinates as uniform and ignores
the final weight conditioning.  These approximations affect the empirical
sample complexity, but not the algebraic certificate applied once a candidate
subspace has been found.

Let $\alpha=k_1/m$ and $\beta=1-\alpha$.  In the submitted $Z$ decoder, a
coordinate is among $k_2$ free positions with probability $k_2/m$; the free
weight is uniform in $\{0,\ldots,k_2\}$, giving mean nonzero probability
$1/2$.  A solved coordinate is modeled as uniform in $\F_3$.  The approximate
zero probability of one $e_2$ coordinate is consequently
\begin{equation}
 \zeta=\frac{k_2}{m}\frac12+
       \frac{m-k_2}{m}\frac13
       =0.3883161512.
 \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}

Normalize the unknown relative scale of a true public pair so that its
dominant nonzero relation is equality.  The parameter-derived joint model is
\begin{equation}
 P\approx
 \begin{pmatrix}
  0.04270143&0.03363210&0.03363210\\
  0.03363210&0.23857772&0.17280736\\
  0.03363210&0.17280736&0.23857772
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
For the null model we use the public fixed-weight marginal
\[
          p_X=\left(\frac{n-w}{n},
                    \frac{w}{2n},\frac{w}{2n}\right)
              =(0.11,0.445,0.445).
\]

For public coordinates $a,b$ and relative scale
$\epsilon\in\{1,2\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 L_{a,b}(\epsilon)=
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}
 \log\frac{P(X_a^{(t)},\epsilon X_b^{(t)})}
               {p_X(X_a^{(t)})p_X(\epsilon X_b^{(t)})}.
 \label{eq:llr}
\end{equation}
The score of $\{a,b\}$ is the larger of the two values.  Each coordinate
selects its highest-scoring partner.  We retain only mutual selections and
sort them by score, avoiding the need to commit immediately to a noisy
perfect matching.

\section{Exact public completion}

\subsection{The pair-sum subspace}

Let $h_i\in\F_3^m$ denote public column $i$.  After accounting for the hidden
monomial map, the two columns belonging to hidden position $j$ can be written
\[
 a_j=c_jS\binom{x_j}{-z_j},
 \qquad
 b_j=d_jS\binom{y_j}{z_j},
\]
for nonzero $c_j,d_j\in\F_3$, an invertible row map $S$, and columns
$x_j,y_j,z_j$ of the secret matrices.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 a_j+\lambda_j b_j
   =c_jS\binom{x_j+y_j}{0}
   \in U,
 \qquad
 \lambda_j=c_jd_j^{-1},
 \label{eq:pairsum}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 U=S\left(\F_3^{r_1}\times\{0\}^{r_2}\right)
       \subset\F_3^m,
 \qquad \dim U=r_1.
\]
The relative scale selected by the likelihood score determines the
combination in \eqref{eq:pairsum}.  Correct high-confidence candidates can
therefore reveal $U$ by ordinary row reduction.

\subsection{Quotient recovery of every pair}

Put a row-reduced basis of $U$ into systematic form $[I_{r_1}\mid A]$ after
partitioning the ambient coordinates into pivot positions $P$ and free
positions $F$.  Define the quotient map
\begin{equation}
 Q(v)=v_F-v_PA\in\F_3^{r_2}.
 \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
Its kernel is exactly $U$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:pairsum} implies
\begin{equation}
              Q(a_j)=-\lambda_j Q(b_j).
 \label{eq:qpair}
\end{equation}
Normalize each nonzero quotient vector by multiplying its first nonzero entry
to one.  The two members of a hidden pair then have identical normalized
quotients.  On the tested public key, the 9,700 columns split into exactly
4,850 normalized classes of size two.  The unnormalized quotient vectors
also give $\lambda_j$.

Once $U$ is correct, this completion stage is exact rather than heuristic:
it uses only arithmetic over $\F_3$.  Conversely, an incorrect candidate
subspace normally produces singleton classes or ambiguous multiplicities,
which are visible from public data.

\subsection{A target-secret-independent stopping rule}

The following procedure uses no secret values:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
  \item collect signatures in fixed batches and reconstruct their complete
        public errors with \eqref{eq:reconstruct};
  \item compute mutual likelihood candidates with \eqref{eq:llr};
  \item take the first $r_1=1600$ candidates and require their oriented sums
        to have rank $r_1$;
  \item construct $Q$ and require every normalized quotient class to contain
        exactly two public columns;
  \item recover the equivalent matrices, decode a fresh hash syndrome, and
        accept the result only if the intended public verifier accepts.
\end{enumerate}
If any condition fails, the attacker collects the next batch and repeats the
procedure.  The experiment used checkpoints at 200 and 300 signatures.  The 200-signature checkpoint
reached rank 1,600 but failed the quotient partition with a singleton class.
The 300-signature checkpoint passed every condition.  The stopping decision
therefore did not inspect the target secret.

\section{Equivalent signing-key construction}

\begin{theorem}[Equivalent UVW form]
\label{thm:equiv}
Assume that $U$, the hidden pairs, and their relative scales have been
recovered as above.  From public information one can construct an invertible
row map $T$ and matrices
\[
 \widetilde H_X,\widetilde H_Y\in\F_3^{r_1\times m},
 \qquad
 \widetilde H_Z\in\F_3^{r_2\times m}
\]
such that, after reordering and relatively scaling paired columns,
\begin{equation}
       T H_{\pk}^{\star}=
       \begin{pmatrix}
        \widetilde H_X&\widetilde H_Y\\
        -\widetilde H_Z&\widetilde H_Z
       \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\widetilde H_X+\widetilde H_Y$ has row rank $r_1$ and
$\widetilde H_Z$ has row rank $r_2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the pivot/free coordinates above and define
\[
                  T(v)=(v_P,\,Q(v)).
\]
This map is invertible because $v$ is recovered from $v_P$ together with
$v_F=Q(v)+v_PA$.  Scale the second member of every recovered
pair so that its sum with the first lies in $U$.  Their quotient components
are then opposites by \eqref{eq:qpair}.  The bottom $r_2$ rows consequently
have the form $(-\widetilde H_Z\mid\widetilde H_Z)$, after a harmless common
scale and pair ordering.  The top rows define
$(\widetilde H_X\mid\widetilde H_Y)$.

The pair sums span $U$, so their top coordinates have rank $r_1$; these are
the columns of $\widetilde H_X+\widetilde H_Y$.  The public matrix has full
row rank, hence its column images span $\F_3^m/U$ in the quotient.  Those
images are the columns of $\widetilde H_Z$ up to signs and scales, so
$\widetilde H_Z$ has rank $r_2$.
\end{proof}

For a target public syndrome $s$, compute
$T(s)=(\widetilde s_1\mid\widetilde s_2)$.  Apply the published decoder
equations
\begin{align*}
 e_2\widetilde H_Z^{\mathsf T}&=\widetilde s_2,\\
 e_1(\widetilde H_X+\widetilde H_Y)^{\mathsf T}
   &=\widetilde s_1-e_2\widetilde H_Y^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align*}
When the ordinary weight rejection returns a vector of weight $w$, map
$(e_1\mid e_1+e_2)$ back through the recovered public pairs and scales.

\begin{corollary}[Fresh-message forgery]
The recovered matrices and column map form an equivalent signing key.  They
decode the hash syndrome of an arbitrary message and salt, and the resulting
signature satisfies the intended UVW verification equation.
\end{corollary}

The construction recovers an equivalent decoder rather than the original
key seed, nonces, or necessarily the submitter's exact secret matrices.

\section{End-to-end experiment}

\subsection{Setup and separation from secret diagnostics}

The experiment used the archived UVW-128 reference implementation whose
public tracker archive hash is
\[
\texttt{bbfa8dad5ee57083578b50b9937e773e6158f72646e825da3d3265cc00cb1294}.
\]
The signing and decoding logic was left unchanged.  Pre-existing diagnostic
prints were removed, and a key-generation helper retained the reconstructed
key in memory so that a separate ground-truth file could be written for later
evaluation.  The public attack itself read only the public key and the
reconstructed-error transcript.

The ground-truth map was consulted only after recovery to measure pair
accuracy.
Running the estimator with diagnostic evaluation enabled and disabled
produced byte-identical candidate files with SHA-256
\[
\texttt{c64058d33637865c223ba7d8556b6bbcfce80b2cc92bbf9236642decafb8ba9a}.
\]
Thus access to ground truth did not affect partner selection, relative-scale
selection, the stopping rule, or the forgery.

All timings were measured on Linux with a four-core Intel Core i5-6500 at
3.20 GHz and 16 GiB of RAM.  Key generation took 132.794 seconds and is not
part of the attack cost because the challenger supplies the public key.

\subsection{Transcript collection}

Four signing processes generated 75 distinct chosen-message signatures each.
The slowest process completed in 2,851.507 seconds, giving a transcript wall
time of 47.53 minutes.  Each signature was generated by the submitted signer.  Its salt and
message hash were used to reconstruct the systematic head with
\eqref{eq:reconstruct}; every reconstructed error had the required weight
8,633.

Each child process reinitialized its process-local submission DRNG after the
fork.  Worker $w\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ used the distinct 64-byte seed
\[
       \mathsf{seed}_w[i]=(167+41i+73w)\bmod 256,
       \qquad 0\leq i<64.
\]
These are fixed reproducibility seeds, not four draws from an external
entropy source.
The submitted SM3-based derivation consumes all 64 bytes and gave four
distinct DRNG states.  The salt, the $Z$-decoder information set and free
values, and the $XY$-decoder information set and free values all draw from
the corresponding child-local state.  Thus the four workers used separate pseudorandom streams rather than
cloned post-fork state.  As checks against accidental stream reuse, all 300
reconstructed errors were distinct, and the pooled same-index cross-worker
Pearson correlations of their binary nonzero indicators lay between
$-8.11\mathbin{\cdot}10^{-4}$ and $2.04\mathbin{\cdot}10^{-3}$.

The public key and 300-error transcript have SHA-256 digests
\begin{align*}
 \texttt{ea4978ef4c7afd3dcfcf7bc70ca39830e7f853ccabe9d9bde037f12bc23390ba},\\
 \texttt{5c44e89bf891ebca8c3588f9c498f561593259b963a1ceda24b7f8002d1f6287},
\end{align*}
respectively.

\section{What is rigorous and what is empirical}

The following parts of the attack are exact:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item reconstruction of the full public error from every signature;
  \item the forced-nonzero condition on each selected hidden pair;
  \item membership of correctly scaled pair sums in $U$;
  \item quotient completion once $U$ is known;
  \item Theorem~\ref{thm:equiv} and the resulting decoder; and
  \item acceptance of the fresh-message forgery by the intended verifier.
\end{itemize}

The likelihood distribution in \eqref{eq:model} is heuristic.  It models
solved coordinates as uniform and does not derive the exact joint law after
fixed-weight rejection and information-set invertibility conditioning.  Its
role is only to supply enough correct candidates to span $U$.  The observed
300-signature threshold is likewise empirical and may vary across keys.  The
public quotient partition and final verification provide public checks against
silently accepting an incorrect statistical guess.

\section{Security implication and scope}

The signing queries may use arbitrary distinct messages.  After the
equivalent signing key is constructed, the adversary chooses a fresh message
and salt, hashes them, decodes the new syndrome, and returns the serialized
tail and salt.  This has the form of an EUF-CMA attack.  The concrete
experiment demonstrates a successful fresh-message forgery for one fixed UVW-128 key.

Only one random key was subjected to recovery from a genuine source
transcript, and only UVW-128 was tested end to end.  The attack method was
developed while analyzing that instance.  A target-secret-independent rerun
on the same transcript used the fixed $r_1$ candidate count and public quotient stopping
certificate, but it is not a prospective new-key trial.  No success-rate
claim over random key generation is made.

UVW-256 and UVW-512 use the same forced-pair mechanism.  The algebraic
completion extends directly, while their signature requirements and
practical running times have not been measured.  They are therefore not
claimed broken in this report.

The attack concerns both the specified sampler and the submitted reference
instantiation.  In particular, the source instantiates the $Z$-decoder
weight choice uniformly over $\{0,\ldots,k_2\}$, which is used in the
heuristic score.  A different distribution would require a new likelihood
model, but it would not remove the exact forced-nonzero fact in
Algorithm~5.

The security proof simulates signatures with vectors sampled uniformly from
the public Hamming sphere.  The specification's domain-sampling results
analyze syndromes of vectors already drawn from that distribution.  They do
not establish that the trapdoor decoder's output is close to that same
distribution.  The persistent pair test distinguishes the submitted decoder
output from a uniform fixed-weight vector, so this simulation step requires a
new argument or a redesigned sampler.

\section{Countermeasures}

Changing the fixed monomial permutation or its signs changes only the unknown
matching that the attack must learn.  Increasing the dimensions enlarges
the polynomial-time scoring and quotient computations without removing the source of leakage.

A repair requires a preimage sampler whose output distribution is proven to
be close to a public, key-independent target distribution across many
signatures.
Rejection on total Hamming weight is insufficient when the secret decoder
forces both members of thousands of fixed hidden pairs to be nonzero.  A
revised proof should compare the actual decoder output with the exact
signing-oracle distribution used in the EUF-CMA games, and full-size tests
should include all pairwise public-coordinate contingency tables under one
fixed key.

\section{Conclusion}

UVW-128's monomial map hides coordinate labels, but it does not hide the
repeated statistical dependence created by the $XY$ decoder.  For the tested key, three hundred ordinary signatures exposed enough
high-confidence pairs to span a 1,600-dimensional public subspace.  Quotienting by that subspace recovers
the entire hidden matching and relative scales exactly.  The resulting
matrices form an equivalent UVW signing key and produce a fresh-message
signature accepted by the intended verifier.

In the demonstrated run, query collection took under an hour and the public
offline computation took under one minute on an older four-core desktop.  Its
statistical sample threshold remains a one-key observation, while the algebraic
completion and final forgery are publicly checkable.
