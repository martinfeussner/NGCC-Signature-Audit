\begin{abstract}
CEDRUS+C is a stateless hash-based signature whose bottom few-time component,
FORS+C, reveals one secret leaf and authentication path in each of $k$ fixed
Merkle trees.  Its specification already observes that repeated use of one
bottom address accumulates leaf openings.  We determine the concrete
consequence when signatures at every bottom address are accumulated and the
forger uses the public message-hash inputs accepted by the verifier.
The concrete parameter analysis is limited to the two category-I sets,
CEDRUS+C-160f and CEDRUS+C-160s.

The adversary requests ordinary independently randomized signatures on
distinct chosen messages, buckets them by the complete $h$-bit bottom address,
and retains one opening for each observed address--coordinate--leaf triple.
At a fixed address, openings from different signatures reconstruct the same
ordered tuple of FORS roots and hence the same FORS+C public key.  One honest
strict-valid hypertree suffix for that address therefore authenticates any
covered coordinate mixture.  For a never-signed message, the adversary
enumerates serialized public $(R,\mathsf{ctr}_{\rm FTS})$ values until the
verifier's hash selects a retained address and only covered leaves.  The
verifier can check the resulting hash condition, but cannot check whether $R$
came from the signer's secret $\PRFmsg$ computation.

We give exact first and second coverage moments.  Poissonizing the acquisition
count makes complete-address loads independent, and a concentration bound
yields a fixed public-hash budget and a success lower bound close to
$1-e^{-1}$.  For CEDRUS+C-160f, the all-address optimum has signing mean
$2^{70.928}$, fixed target budget $2^{66.640}$, and $2^{71.000}$ attacker
public-$\Hmsg$ evaluations including transcript parsing.  For 160s the
corresponding exponents are $74.760$, $71.139$, and $74.873$.  Prefix shards
whose fully provisioned tables are below $2^{80}$ bytes give public-hash totals
$2^{71.292}$ and $2^{75.701}$.  Each row has success at least $0.63212$ before
a negligible hard-query-cap abort.

Those interaction counts do not make the attack physically executable.  The
sharded rows return $2^{88.494}$ and $2^{91.795}$ bits and cost the honest
signer $2^{100.156}$ and $2^{108.597}$ measured reference cycles.  Sharding a
fully provisioned table to the audit host's 125.77~GiB raises the public-hash
totals to $2^{73.652}$ and $2^{79.811}$ and signer work to $2^{102.474}$ and
$2^{112.010}$ cycles.  We report every resource in its own unit.  No
full-parameter end-to-end attack was executed.

A scaled experiment under the strict repaired verifier produced an accepted
fresh-message forgery from ordinary randomized signatures and rejected seven
adversarial controls.
Separate native experiments using the untouched submitted FORS cores confirm
the full-parameter coordinate splice for 160f and 160s.  The attack uses a
strict, scheme-favourable interpretation of the full-width specification and
does not use reset, repeated randomness, deterministic signing, weak or
related keys, omitted WOTS checks, or the submitted source's tree-index
collapse.  The generic
FORS+C accumulation mechanism appears in CEDRUS+C Section~3.2, and public-hash
amplification appears in Abri--Katz.  Our claimed contribution is limited to
the CEDRUS+C parameter and lifetime consequence, rigorous all-address
concentration and sharding analysis, and complete resource accounting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

CEDRUS+C is a stateless hash-based signature in the SPHINCS+ family
\cite{cedrus-spec,sphincsc}.  A hypertree authenticates a bottom FORS+C public
key.  The message digest selects a complete bottom address and one leaf in each
of $k$ Merkle trees.  Revealing different leaves under one repeated address
gradually exposes a larger fraction of the possible digest vectors.

This phenomenon is acknowledged in the submission.  Section~3.2 defines the
overall signing-query count $q_{\rm sig}$, mixes over the occupancy of one
FORS+C instance, and gives
\begin{equation}
 p^{\rm FTS}_{\rm Succ}(q)=
 \left[1-(1-2^{-a})^q\right]^k2^{-a'} .              \label{eq:submitted}
\end{equation}
That expression concerns a new random target after $q$ uses of one instance.
The specification does not turn it into an all-address attack, control the
realized aggregate table, optimize signing and public target trials together,
or state the resulting resource points for its parameters.

The serialized message randomizer supplies the missing public target search.
Algorithm~18 reads $R$ and a public counter from the candidate signature and
recomputes $\Hmsg$.  Verification has no secret input with which to prove that
$R$ originated from $\PRFmsg$.  An ordinary forger can therefore hold a fresh
message fixed and enumerate public values until the digest is covered by the
accumulated table.  This verifier fact is standard for randomized hash-based
signatures.  Abri and Katz explicitly multiply a related occupancy attack by
the number of public hash queries \cite[Eq.~(2)]{abri-katz}; we do not claim
that technique as new.

The concrete result is an ordinary chosen-message forgery under a strict,
scheme-favourable interpretation of the specification.  It excludes two easier
defects in the submission: the implementation
sets the hypertree index to zero, and the written WOTS+C verifier omits a
membership check.  We instead decode the full tree index and grant the scheme
the strongest coherent WOTS+C checks.  The adversary uses independent hedged
signing randomness and never asks the signer to repeat a random value or state.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item We give an explicit same-address coordinate-splicing forgery and prove
        acceptance by the strict repaired verifier.
  \item We derive exact first and second moments for the fraction of covered
        FORS vectors at one address.  Exact Poisson splitting and Chebyshev
        concentration turn the expected coverage into a one-run success bound.
  \item We optimize all-address and fixed-prefix attacks for the two
        category-I parameter sets, CEDRUS+C-160f and CEDRUS+C-160s, under a
        hard abort before $2^{80}$ signing requests.  The concrete parameter
        analysis is limited to these two sets.
  \item We keep signing queries, target hashes, transcript parsing, returned
        data, attacker tables, table operations, and honest-signer cycles in
        separate units.  We also give rows fitting a fully provisioned table
        in the audit host's RAM.
  \item We validate the complete attack with a scaled strict repaired verifier
        and the splice relation with untouched submitted FORS code at the full
        160f and 160s dimensions.  We make no claim that the astronomical
        full-parameter acquisition was executed.
  \item We delimit novelty to the candidate-specific parameter/lifetime
        consequence and its rigorous concrete accounting.  Equation
        \eqref{eq:submitted} and public-hash amplification are prior art.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Strict, scheme-favourable specification interpretation}

\subsection{Keys, ordinary randomized signing, and digest parsing}

For node length $n$, Algorithms~16--18 define
\begin{equation}
 \sk=(\sk.\mathsf{seed},\sk.\mathsf{prf},
      \pk.\mathsf{seed},\pk.\mathsf{root}),\qquad
 \pk=(\pk.\mathsf{seed},\pk.\mathsf{root}).           \label{eq:keys}
\end{equation}
The first three seeds are independent $n$-byte values, and
$\pk.\mathsf{root}$ is the top hypertree root.  Ordinary hedged signing draws
a fresh $n$-byte value $\mathsf{addrnd}$ and computes
\begin{align}
 R&=\PRFmsg(\sk.\mathsf{prf},\mathsf{addrnd},M),        \label{eq:R}\\
 D&=\Hmsg(R,\pk.\mathsf{seed},\pk.\mathsf{root},
           M,\mathsf{ctr}_{\rm FTS}).                  \label{eq:D}
\end{align}
The signer increments the public 32-bit counter until the designated $a'$ bits
of $D$ are zero.  The remaining disjoint fields select $k$ independent
$a$-bit FORS indices and the complete bottom address
\begin{equation}
 A=(\mathsf{layer}=0,\mathsf{idxTree},\mathsf{idxLeaf}),
 \qquad |\mathsf{idxTree}|+|\mathsf{idxLeaf}|=h .       \label{eq:address}
\end{equation}
In the ideal-$\Hmsg$ model used by the submission, conditioning on the
disjoint forced field leaves $A$ uniform on $2^h$ values and all FORS indices
uniform and independent.  Independent hedged signing queries therefore give
independent samples.  Table~\ref{tab:params} records the two lowest parameter
sets.

\subsection{The fixed-address FORS+C relation}

Put $B=2^a$.  For coordinate $i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$, leaf
$x\in\{0,\ldots,B-1\}$, and global leaf number $u=iB+x$, Algorithms~12--15
define
\begin{align}
 s_{A,i,x}&=\PRF(\pk.\mathsf{seed},\sk.\mathsf{seed},
              \mathsf{ADRS}_{\rm FORS\mbox{-}PRF}(A,u)),       \label{eq:secret}\\
 L_{A,i,x}&=F(\pk.\mathsf{seed},
              \mathsf{ADRS}_{\rm FORS\mbox{-}TREE}(A,u,0),
              s_{A,i,x}).                                    \label{eq:leaf}
\end{align}
The $B$ leaves for coordinate $i$ form a height-$a$ Merkle tree with root
$T_{A,i}$.  The address-specific FORS+C public key is
\begin{equation}
 P_A=T_k(\pk.\mathsf{seed},\mathsf{ADRS}_{\rm FORS\mbox{-}ROOT}(A),
          T_{A,0}\concat\cdots\concat T_{A,k-1}).              \label{eq:forspk}
\end{equation}
A signature selecting $x_i$ reveals $s_{A,i,x_i}$ and its complete
authentication path.  The path reconstructs the fixed root $T_{A,i}$.

Algorithm~17 signs $P_A$ with the bottom WOTS+C key at $A$.  At every higher
layer, the WOTS+C message is likewise a fixed structural root.  A complete
hypertree suffix from an honest signature at $A$ therefore authenticates
exactly the same value whenever a mixed FORS opening reconstructs $P_A$.

\subsection{Strict WOTS+C and full-address interpretation}

The submitted source's collapsed tree index is not used.  We parse the full
$h-h_0$ tree bits and the full $h_0$ leaf bits in Algorithm~18, so there are
exactly $2^h$ bottom addresses.

We also grant CEDRUS+C the strongest coherent interpretation of its WOTS+C
verification.  Each serialized WOTS counter is installed in the root-hash
address; the verifier checks both the leading-zero predicate and the fixed-sum
digit-vector membership predicate before reconstructing the WOTS key.  This
strict check only strengthens the scheme.  The accumulation attack copies one
complete valid suffix without altering any WOTS chain.  Moreover, all legal
digit vectors have the same sum.  If public chain advancement maps legal
$x$ to legal $y$, then $y_j\geq x_j$ for every coordinate; equal sums force
$x=y$.  Thus no omitted WOTS check or chain-advancement shortcut contributes
to the result.

Minor naming and serialization slips in the written algorithms are resolved
in the scheme's favor: the one-counter layout shown by Figure~1.14,
Algorithm~18's length, published sizes, and source is used; stale PORS/FORS
names denote Algorithms~12--15; and the defined bottom-to-top hypertree flow is
used.  None of these repairs creates the fixed value $P_A$.

\section{Coordinate-splicing forgery}

\begin{lemma}[Same-address coordinate mixing]\label{lem:mix}
Fix an ordinary key and complete address $A$.  For every target vector
$x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1})$, one valid opening of $(A,i,x_i)$ may be selected
independently for each coordinate $i$ from different signatures.  Their
ordered combination reconstructs $P_A$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:forspk}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every selected component reconstructs the fixed root $T_{A,i}$ of its own
coordinate tree.  Verification preserves coordinate order and compresses the
same tuple $(T_{A,0},\ldots,T_{A,k-1})$ under the same root address.  The result
is therefore $P_A$, independently of which donor supplied each component.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Public target inputs]\label{lem:public}
For a fixed public key and message $M^*$, a forger may submit arbitrary
serialized values $(R^*,\mathsf{ctr}^*)$ to Algorithm~18 and is accepted when
the recomputed digest satisfies the public forced-bit, address, and opening
checks.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Algorithm~18 parses both values from the signature and recomputes
Equation~\eqref{eq:D}.  It has neither $\sk.\mathsf{prf}$ nor
$\mathsf{addrnd}$ and therefore cannot test Equation~\eqref{eq:R}.  Signature
validity is determined by the public verification checks.  The 160-bit $R$
field alone supplies more distinct inputs than any target budget below.
\end{proof}

The adversary proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Draw an acquisition count $N\sample\Pois(2^q)$.  Abort rather than
        issue signing request $2^{80}$.  Otherwise request $N$ ordinary hedged
        signatures on distinct messages.
  \item Recompute each public digest, bucket the signature by its exact
        complete address $A$, and retain one opening for every observed
        $(A,i,x)$.  Retain one complete strict-valid hypertree suffix per used
        address.  A fixed high-bit prefix may be selected in advance to shard
        the table.
  \item Fix a message $M^*$ never submitted to the signing oracle.  Enumerate
        distinct public $(R^*,\mathsf{ctr}^*)$ values until the forced field is
        zero, the selected address is retained, and every selected
        $(i,x_i^*)$ has an opening.
  \item For each coordinate copy a matching opening, then attach the retained
        suffix for the same complete address.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Fresh-message forgery]\label{thm:forge}
The attack output is accepted by the strict repaired verifier
on the never-queried message $M^*$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The verifier derives the target vector and address from the serialized public
inputs and $M^*$.  Every coordinate opening is valid at that exact address.
Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} makes their ordered combination reconstruct $P_A$.  The
copied strict-valid suffix authenticates this unchanged $P_A$ to
$\pk.\mathsf{root}$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:public} permits the chosen public inputs.
All checks pass, and $M^*$ is fresh by construction.
\end{proof}

Cross-address mixing does not satisfy this proof.  The full tree and keypair
fields remain in the compressed address, while secret generation, leaf
hashing, tree hashing, root compression, and the bottom WOTS key are address
separated.  The experiment includes a wrong-complete-address rejection
control.

\section{Exact coverage and concentration}

\subsection{Coverage at one address}

Suppose an address receives $r$ ordinary signatures.  For one coordinate, the
expected fraction of covered leaves is
\begin{equation}
 g_r=1-(1-1/B)^r.                                      \label{eq:g}
\end{equation}
Let $Z_A$ be the fraction of all $B^k$ coordinate vectors covered at address
$A$.  Conditional independence of the $k$ disjoint digest fields gives
\begin{equation}
 \E[Z_A\mid r]=g_r^k.                                  \label{eq:first}
\end{equation}

For the second moment, select two target leaves independently in one
coordinate.  With probability $1/B$ they agree and have joint coverage
$g_r$; otherwise both distinct leaves have appeared with probability
$1-2(1-1/B)^r+(1-2/B)^r$.  Hence
\begin{align}
 s_r&=\frac{g_r}{B}+\left(1-\frac1B\right)
   \left[1-2(1-1/B)^r+(1-2/B)^r\right],                \label{eq:s}\\
 \E[Z_A^2\mid r]&=s_r^k.                               \label{eq:second}
\end{align}
These are exact occupancy identities rather than sparse-Poisson
approximations.

\subsection{Exact Poisson splitting}

Drawing $N\sim\Pois(2^q)$ signatures and assigning each independently to one
of $2^h$ addresses makes all address loads independent
$\Pois(\lambda)$ variables, exactly, where
\begin{equation}
                         \lambda=2^{q-h}.               \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
Write $\mu=\E[Z_A]$ and $\nu=\E[Z_A^2]$.  Direct Poisson summation of
Equations~\eqref{eq:first} and~\eqref{eq:second} gives both quantities.  An
independent closed-form check follows from the Poisson probability-generating
function.  Put
\[
 \alpha=1-B^{-1},\quad \beta=1-2B^{-1},\quad
 A_0=2-B^{-1},\quad C_0=1-B^{-1}.
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \mu&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j \binom{k}{j}
      \exp\!\left(\lambda(\alpha^j-1)\right),            \label{eq:muclosed}\\
 \nu&=\sum_{i+j+\ell=k}\frac{k!}{i!j!\ell!}
      (-A_0)^i C_0^j
      \exp\!\left(\lambda(\alpha^i\beta^j-1)\right).    \label{eq:nuclosed}
\end{align}
The release computes the direct sum with an explicit omitted-tail bound below
$2^{-400}$ and evaluates the closed form independently at high precision.  The
four headline moment pairs agree within $3.2\cdot10^{-13}$ bits.

\subsection{Shard mass and a one-run success bound}

Preselect a prefix of $b$ address bits, retaining
$M=2^{h-b}$ addresses.  Conditional on the realized table, one raw public
target trial succeeds with probability
\begin{equation}
 p(\mathcal T)=2^{-a'}2^{-h}\sum_{A\in\mathcal T}Z_A,
 \qquad \E[p]=2^{-a'-b}\mu.                             \label{eq:trial}
\end{equation}
Poisson splitting makes the retained $Z_A$ independent.  Since
$\Var(Z_A)\leq\nu$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Var(\sum_A Z_A)}{\E[\sum_A Z_A]^2}
 \leq 2^{b-h}\frac{\nu}{\mu^2}.                        \label{eq:relvar}
\end{equation}
Chebyshev therefore bounds the probability that the table has less than half
its mean mass by
\begin{equation}
 \delta\leq4\,2^{b-h}\frac{\nu}{\mu^2}.                \label{eq:cheb}
\end{equation}
On the complementary event, fix
\begin{equation}
 T=\left\lceil\frac{2}{\E[p]}\right\rceil .             \label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
Then $p(\mathcal T)T\geq1$, so independent ideal-$\Hmsg$ target trials fail
with probability at most $e^{-1}$.  Before the acquisition-cap abort, success
is at least
\begin{equation}
                     (1-\delta)(1-e^{-1}).              \label{eq:success}
\end{equation}
Every displayed acquisition mean is below $2^{80}$.  The attack aborts before
request $2^{80}$, and the Poisson upper-tail correction is negligible.  Even
for the largest mean $2^{79}$, the standard bound at twice the mean is
$(e/4)^{2^{79}}$.

\subsection{Storage formulas}

For completeness, an address with mean occupancy $\lambda$ has expected
distinct leaves per coordinate
\begin{equation}
 D=B(1-e^{-\lambda/B}).                                  \label{eq:distinct}
\end{equation}
At subtree height $j$, the expected number of empty subtrees whose sibling is
nonempty is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{B}{2^j}\left(e^{-\lambda2^j/B}
                    -e^{-\lambda2^{j+1}/B}\right).       \label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:frontier} gives the compact authentication
frontier.  The release verifies this identity exhaustively for a small tree.
The complete record occupies $((a+1)n+1)$ bytes, and a copied hypertree suffix
occupies
\begin{equation}
 S=d(4+\mathsf{len}\,n)+hn                         \label{eq:suffix}
\end{equation}
bytes before small table metadata.  These formulas reproduce every table
exponent above.  Simple sharing of identical upper hypertree suffix chunks
changes constants but does not reduce a prefix exponent.

\section{Experimental validation}

\subsection{Scaled strict repaired-verifier forgery}

The executable strict repaired-verifier model retains the relevant
address-separated FORS,
Merkle, WOTS, and message-hash structure at reduced dimensions.  It generated
240 ordinary randomized signatures under one independently generated key and
strictly verified every one.  The attack produced a signature accepted on the
literal message \texttt{fresh-message-never-queried}.  Its three FORS
coordinates came from three distinct signing queries, its target vector had
never appeared at that address, and the complete forged transcript was absent
from the acquired transcripts.

The following negative controls all rejected:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item changing the message;
  \item changing serialized $R$;
  \item changing a FORS authentication node;
  \item violating strict WOTS+C membership;
  \item attaching a hypertree suffix from another address;
  \item substituting a FORS component from another address; and
  \item changing the selected FORS index without changing its opening.
\end{enumerate}
The experiment uses fresh randomized signing and an independently generated
ordinary key.  It does not set, repeat, or roll back signer randomness.

\subsection{Full-parameter native FORS-level splice}

A second test compiles one common harness against the untouched submitted
reference FORS sources for 160f and 160s.  At the exact submitted values
$(n,a,k)=(20,7,30)$ and $(20,12,13)$, it generates three openings at one
complete address, selects a previously unseen coordinate mixture, and
reconstructs the unchanged address-specific public key.  All donor openings
self-verify.  Secret, authentication-path, selected-index, and complete-address
mutations change the root.

These are full-parameter FORS-level conformance tests.  They do not perform
the required address-collision acquisition, public target search, WOTS
verification, or an end-to-end full-parameter forgery.  The submitted source
is useful here because its native FORS core has the fixed-address relation in
Equations~\eqref{eq:secret}--\eqref{eq:forspk}; the known top-level tree-index
bug is outside the harness and supplies no advantage.

\subsection{Independent hostile reconstruction}

The hostile review used an independent direct Poisson recurrence and did not
import the main resource analyzer.  For the four rows in
Table~\ref{tab:headline}, it reproduced $\log_2\mu$, $\log_2T$, and
$\log_2(Q+T)$ within $6\cdot10^{-12}$ bits.  A second implementation evaluated
Equations~\eqref{eq:muclosed}--\eqref{eq:nuclosed}, checked exact WOTS+C
membership probabilities by integer dynamic programming, and exhaustively
checked the compact-forest formula.  Fresh native rebuilds at 160f and 160s
matched the prior outputs.

The review's verdict was ``valid abstract attack, not host scale.''  It found
no hidden reset, randomness collision, weak-key condition, address
transplantation, WOTS shortcut, source-only dependency, or uncounted physical
resource.  The release preserves that verdict and its attack ledger.

\section{Scope, lifetime, and prior art}

\subsection{What is and is not claimed}

The result is an ordinary fresh-message forgery in the ideal-$\Hmsg$ model
used by the submission.  The acquisition oracle returns independently
randomized hedged signatures.  The attack needs none of the following:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item reset, rollback, repeated signer state, or repeated $R$;
  \item deterministic signing, a chosen-randomness interface, or a fault;
  \item related keys, weak keys, or a rare hash/key condition;
  \item the submitted implementation's collapsed tree index; or
  \item Algorithm~6's omitted strict WOTS+C membership check.
\end{itemize}
The result also does not say that the full-parameter attack was executed or is
currently physically practical.  Only the scaled end-to-end forgery and
full-parameter FORS-level splice were run.

\subsection{Per-key lifetime}

An exhaustive text audit covered the 53-page specification, the English and
Chinese basic-information forms, all reference and optimized source trees,
all 16 implementation READMEs, and the security-claim material.  It found no
maximum signatures-per-key value, mandatory key rotation, or per-key lifetime
rule.  Section~3.2 instead introduces $q_{\rm sig}$ as ``the number of signing
queries overall'' and expresses the forgery probability as a function of
$q_{\rm sig}$ in Equations~(3.1)--(3.2), without stating a numerical upper
bound on $q_{\rm sig}$.
The separate NGCC Submission Requirements require each key pair to support
signing and verifying at least $2^{64}$ different messages
\cite{ngcc-requirements}; this is a minimum support requirement, not a maximum
per-key lifetime.  The NGCC Evaluation Criteria separately permit the attacker
to have access to signatures on no more than $2^{80}$ chosen messages
\cite{ngcc-criteria}.

The precise conclusion is that the specification states no per-key lifetime
cap that excludes the attack points reported here, while their signing-query
counts remain below the official NGCC chosen-message evaluation bound.  This
does not attribute an affirmative unlimited-lifetime policy to the submitters.
A deployment explicitly and enforceably retiring a key after $2^{64}$
signatures would fall outside the attack range established here.

\subsection{Prior art and novelty boundary}

CEDRUS+C Section~3.2 already describes repeated-instance FORS+C accumulation
and gives Equation~\eqref{eq:submitted}.  SPHINCS+C introduced the related
counter-selected FORS+C design and its security setting \cite{sphincsc}.
Abri--Katz integrate over few-time-key occupancy and explicitly multiply by a
public hash-query count \cite{abri-katz}.  We claim neither generic leaf
accumulation, public replacement of a serialized counter/randomizer, nor the
public-query multiplier as new.

At the 3~October~2026 checkpoint, the public CEDRUS+C page listed only the
distinct submitted-code tree-index-collapse attack \cite{ngcc-cedrus}.  This
is a dated, best-effort statement about public reports, not proof that no
unpublished or unindexed analysis exists.

The narrow contribution is the candidate-specific result: a coordinate-splicing
algorithm accepted under the strict repaired verifier, exact all-address
first and second moments, a concentration-controlled public-target budget,
prefix sharding and concrete $q_S/q_H$ optimization for the submitted
category-I parameters, a submission-wide lifetime audit, and resource tables
that expose rather than suppress the dominant physical costs.

\section{Mitigation}

The direct operational mitigation is a per-key signature limit chosen jointly
with the public-hash budget.  A stated $2^{64}$ lifetime excludes every attack
point reported here.  Parameter selection should evaluate the aggregate
success over all bottom addresses and all permitted public target trials,
rather than only the success of one random target at one instance.  The same
analysis should include returned data, table memory, and honest signing cost
when making a practical-security claim.

Changing only the implementation tree-index bug does not repair the attack
under the strict repaired-verifier model; full-width indexing is already
assumed.  Likewise,
adding the omitted WOTS+C membership check is sound but irrelevant to the
splice, which preserves one complete valid WOTS/hypertree suffix.

\section{Conclusion}

Ordinary randomized CEDRUS+C signatures expose reusable FORS+C coordinate
openings whenever their complete bottom addresses agree.  Accumulating those
openings over all addresses, then grinding verifier-controlled public message
hash inputs, yields a fresh-message forgery with constant success probability.
The strict 160f and 160s interaction exponents are approximately $71.0$ and
$74.9$ for all-address tables, or $71.3$ and $75.7$ when deterministic table
payload is kept below $2^{80}$ bytes.

These figures establish a parameter/lifetime-policy issue under the NGCC
evaluation model, not a machine-practical computation.  Returned traffic and
honest signer work are much larger; actual-host RAM sharding still leaves
trillion-year or quadrillion-year signing estimates under idealized scaling.
The attack's structural core and probability bounds survive independent
hostile reconstruction, a scaled strict repaired-verifier forgery, and native
full-parameter FORS splice tests.  Its generic ingredients remain prior art;
the new claim is limited to their rigorous CEDRUS+C consequence.
